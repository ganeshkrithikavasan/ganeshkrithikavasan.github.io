How I Think About AI Products

The real problem isn't AI capability. It's product thinking.

Most enterprise AI products are bolt-on intelligence layered over broken workflows. The opportunity — and the hard work — is in reimagining the workflow itself. That's where durable product value lives.

01

Shift from action to review — not from slow to fast.

The most powerful AI products don't make users do things faster. They remove the need for users to initiate the action at all. Turning filing, tagging, classifying, and routing into a review process changes adoption trajectories entirely.

02

Behavior change is the actual product. The model is infrastructure.

In enterprise AI, most failed rollouts are adoption failures, not technical failures. The PM who designs for behavior change — not just feature delivery — ships products that survive contact with real users.

03

Domain expertise × AI fluency is the scarcest skill combination.

Most AI PMs don't understand enterprise workflows deeply. Most domain experts don't understand AI well enough to make real product decisions. The gap between those two is where the highest-leverage product work lives.

04

Enterprise AI metrics are still being invented. That's the opportunity.

Accuracy and recall are model metrics. Time-to-value, friction reduction, and workflow displacement rate are product metrics. The PMs who define what "good" means for AI products in enterprise will set the standard for a decade.

About Me

14 years at the intersection of enterprise software and human behavior — and AI just made that infinitely more interesting.

My career started in R&D, which gave me something most PMs don't have: a genuine understanding of what's hard technically, and why that matters when you're making product decisions under pressure.

Over the past 8 years I've focused on enterprise knowledge work products — software that helps professionals in law, finance, and business operations manage the documents, contracts, and communications that run their organizations.

I've shipped 6 zero-to-one products, 4 of them in legaltech. The through-line across all of them: the hardest problem was never the technology. It was always getting smart, busy people to change how they work. AI is finally making that solvable.

I write about AI product strategy, enterprise workflow design, and the patterns I've noticed across industries. If any of it resonates — let's talk.
